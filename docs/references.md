# Area realism (workstream D)

Reference notes for building/street archetypes. Units: m unless stated (1 ft = 0.3048 m).
"(unverified)" = from general knowledge or a single weak source; check against OSM/Street View before hard-coding.
Facts without the tag came from the sources listed at the end of each section.

## 0. Quick numbers (cheat sheet)

- Storey height: 19th-c. commercial ground floor 4.5-5.5 m, upper floors 3.6-4.2 m; houses 2.7-3.0 m per floor; 1920s towers ~4.3 m/floor.
- Downtown Davenport right-of-way (ROW) = 80 ft (24.4 m). Travel lanes ~11 ft (3.35 m) now; plan target 10 ft (3.05 m), 11 ft on truck routes. Parallel parking 8 x 22 ft (2.4 x 6.7 m).
- Downtown Davenport streetlights: 2 ft (0.6 m) back of curb, every 44 ft (13.4 m), staggered across the street. Furniture is black, matte.
- Crosswalks downtown Davenport: painted, 10 ft (3.05 m) wide, lined up with the building faces.
- Rock Island brick streets: 25.3-37 ft (7.7-11.3 m) curb to curb; Highland Park bricks 25.3-25.6 ft (~7.8 m).
- Residential street, older grid: 28-31 ft (8.5-9.5 m) curb to curb, 60 ft (18.3 m) ROW, no centre line (unverified, typical US Midwest).
- Corn rows 30 in (0.76 m); county road grid every 1 mile (1.609 km). Scott County: 324 mi gravel vs 225 mi paved county roads.
- Power poles: urban 35-45 m apart, rural distribution 60-90 m (unverified, typical US).

## 1. Downtown Davenport

**Street layout and traffic**
- Grid of numbered streets running east-west (River Dr, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th...), crossed by named north-south streets (Perry, Brady, Main, Harrison, Ripley, Scott, Western...). Main St is the dividing line between E and W addresses.
- One-way history: 3rd and 4th St were made one-way in 1954, Brady and Harrison in 1972-73.
- **3rd St became two-way on 6 Nov 2025** from Marquette St to River Dr: one lane each way plus a centre turn lane.
- **4th St**: being resurfaced and converted to two-way, due fall 2026. For a "present day" build, model it as two-way 1+1 with a centre turn lane. Before conversion: 3rd ran westbound, 4th eastbound (unverified directions).
- **Brady St = one-way northbound, Harrison St = one-way southbound** (the US 61 pair), still one-way. Studies recommend fewer lanes plus parking; nothing is scheduled. Typically 3 lanes plus parking (unverified).
- **2nd St**: two-way, with angled (diagonal) parking on parts of E 2nd St (the plan photo shows it; the plan says replace with parallel parking where feasible).
- **River Drive** = US 67 / state highway. Seen as a barrier between downtown and the riverfront. 4 lanes, with turn lanes at intersections and a partial median (unverified). Streetlights sit at the back of the sidewalk.
- Main St south of River Dr was closed in 1997 to make the plaza at the John Dillon Memorial (fountain).
- Elevated Rock Island railroad tracks (built 1901-03) run along 5th St; they mark the north edge of downtown.
- Speed limit mostly 25 mph (40 km/h).

**Buildings**
- Eras: 1850s-1968. Mostly 1870-1931 brick and terracotta commercial blocks; 1950s-60s civic and modern buildings; 1990s-2000s infill (MidAmerican 1995, Figge 2005).
- Styles: Italianate and Victorian commercial blocks (2-4 storeys), Chicago-school and Beaux-Arts office towers (7-17 storeys), Art Deco/Moderne (Adler 1931, US Courthouse 1933), mid-century modern (Scott County Courthouse, library 1968).
- Materials: red and buff brick, cream/white glazed terracotta on the 1910s-20s towers, limestone bases; dark glass on the modern towers.
- Setback: 0 m. Buildings sit on the property line, and the sidewalk runs from kerb to facade.
- Lot widths: 20-30 ft (6-9 m) for the old blocks; corner towers fill 1/4 to 1/2 of a block (unverified).
- Tower heights (Wikipedia list; single-source):
  - Davenport Bank & Trust (now Wells Fargo), 1927: 78 m, 17 floors. Tallest in the Quad Cities.
  - MidAmerican Building, 1995: 66 m, 15 floors.
  - Kahl Building / Capitol Theatre, 1919-22: 44 m, 10 floors.
  - US Bank Building, 1923: 44 m, 10 floors.
  - Hotel Blackhawk, 1915: 42 m, 11 floors.
  - Mississippi Lofts / Adler Theatre (old RKO Orpheum), 1931: 40 m, 10 floors.
  - Putnam Building, 1910: 38 m, 8 floors.
  - Union Arcade, 1924: 38 m, 7 floors.
  - Sacred Heart Cathedral (49 m) and Trinity Cathedral (40 m) are spires just outside the core.

**Sidewalk and furniture (2018 Streetscape Plan, adopted standard)**
- Sidewalk = broom-finished concrete in 4-5 ft "window-pane" panels with smooth troweled edges.
- On primary streets, a brick accent strip runs along the kerb: 4 ft x 35 ft (1.2 x 10.7 m), 4x8 in pavers in 90-degree herringbone, with a darker border (stretcher + header course).
- Tree wells 4 x 9 ft (1.2 x 2.7 m), placed between parking stalls and staggered across the street, with grates or low black fencing.
- Primary streets (incl. Main, 2nd, 3rd): brick strip + concrete + decorative lights + trees + 8 ft (2.4 m) curb bulb-outs at corners. Secondary streets: concrete + lights + trees, no brick. River Dr: concrete + lights only.
- Lights: black decorative LED poles replacing cobra heads, with hanging flower baskets in season (Harrison St has them) and seasonal banners (unverified).
- Benches: black metal strap. Bins: black metal, plus small bins on utility poles at corners. Bollards about 3 ft (0.9 m). Pedestrian clear path at least 7 ft (2.1 m).

**Riverfront**
- LeClaire Park: about 400 acres of riverfront parkland. The W.D. Petersen Memorial Music Pavilion ("LeClaire Park Bandshell", 1924) is a white arched bandshell facing a lawn (unverified look). It hosts the Mississippi Valley Blues Fest and the Bix jazz festival.
- No permanent flood wall: Davenport famously has no levee, so the riverfront floods. The "levee" is a riverside park strip and rec path. Riverfront Parkway runs through the park.
- Riverfront rec path: a ~3 m concrete/asphalt multi-use trail along the river (the Mississippi River Trail) (unverified width).
- Skybridge (2005): walkway 575 ft (175 m) long, deck 50 ft (15.2 m) above River Dr, mast 99 ft (30 m). Lit with changing LED colours at night (unverified). Runs from the 2nd St parking ramp courtyard (by the River Music Experience) to LeClaire Park.
- Modern Woodmen Park (1931 brick ballpark, River Bandits), west of LeClaire Park. Ferris wheel and corn-maze-style outfield (unverified).
- Centennial Bridge (1940): a run of steel tied arches to Rock Island, starting at Gaines St/River Dr. Government Bridge (1896 swing bridge to Arsenal Island) is at the east end of downtown (unverified detail).

**Local spots (name, street)**
- Figge Art Museum: 225 W 2nd St. Glass-box facade by David Chipperfield, 2005 (unverified address).
- Hotel Blackhawk: 200 E 3rd St (unverified address).
- Adler Theatre: 136 E 3rd St (unverified address).
- Capitol Theatre (Kahl Bldg): 330 W 3rd St (unverified address).
- Front Street Brewery: 208 E River Dr (unverified address).
- Raccoon Motel (music venue): 315 E 2nd St (unverified address).
- River Music Experience (Redstone Building): 129 Main St (unverified address).
- Freight House (farmers market + Front Street Brewery taproom): 421 W River Dr (unverified address). Market is year-round, Sat/Sun, 200+ vendors.
- John Dillon Memorial: plaza at Main St & River Dr.
- City Hall (1895, Romanesque with clock tower): 226 W 4th St (unverified address).
- Davenport Public Library main branch (1968): 321 Main St (unverified address).
- Main Street Landing: riverfront plaza at the foot of Main St.
- Duck City Bistro: E 3rd St (unverified address).

## 2. Village of East Davenport

- Location: about 2.5 km east of downtown along E 11th-13th St, Mound St, Christie St and Jersey Ridge Rd, between River Dr and the bluff (unverified bounds).
- NRHP 1980, 145 contributing buildings, mostly working-class houses. Building stock 1848-1910, many Civil War era.
- Commercial: 1-3 storey brick storefronts and frame buildings with false fronts, mixed with small houses. Narrow lots, 25-40 ft (7.6-12 m). Shops sit at 0 m setback; houses are set back 2-5 m (unverified).
- Materials: soft red brick, painted clapboard (white, cream, sage, barn red), limestone foundations, bracketed Italianate cornices (unverified style mix, typical of the period).
- Streets: narrow two-lane streets with parallel parking. Steep grade north up Mound St and Jersey Ridge (unverified). Old-style black lamp posts and seasonal banners (unverified).
- 1874 railroad trestle still stands on the west side.
- Local spots:
  - Lagomarcino's (chocolates, soda fountain): 2132 E 11th St.
  - Bayside Bistro: 1105 Christie St, across from Lagomarcino's.
  - East Village Preservation Society: 1018 Mound St.

  - East Turner Hall, now the Village Theatre: E 11th St (unverified address).
  - Former Kuehl Hotel; Village Market Place.
  - Lindsay Park: E River Dr at the Village edge. Bandstand and Camp McClellan marker (unverified bandstand).
  - Former Pierce School No. 13 (NRHP).
  - Bix 7 race course passes through (unverified).

## 4. Davenport neighbourhoods

**General**
- Grid of 300 ft blocks (unverified), with rear alleys in most pre-1940 areas (Near North Side confirmed "in portions").
- Houses close to the street with detached garages at the back. Alleys are concrete, gravel or old brick, about 3-5 m wide (unverified).
- Overhead power and phone lines usually run along the alley where one exists, otherwise along the street at the back of the parkway. Wooden poles 35-45 m apart (unverified, typical).
- Parkway (grass strip) 1.5-3 m between kerb and a 1.2-1.5 m concrete sidewalk, with street trees (maples, oaks, ash replaced after emerald ash borer) (unverified).
- Lots: 40-50 ft (12-15 m) wide x 120-150 ft (37-46 m) deep (unverified). Front setback 4.5-9 m. Side gaps 1.5-3 m.
- Brick streets: Davenport requires some streets to stay brick and budgets for upkeep, but no list was found. McClellan Heights keeps a few in excellent condition. Others are in parts of Hamburg/Gold Coast and around Vander Veer (unverified). Mostly red-brown pavers (Purington-style).

**West End / Hamburg / Gold Coast (W of downtown, on the bluff)**
- Hamburg Historic District: German, 1850-1900. Brick Italianate and Greek Revival townhouses and cottages, 2-3 storeys, often only 0-3 m from the sidewalk, on steep hillside streets.
- Gold Coast: big 1850s-1900 Italianate, Second Empire and Queen Anne mansions on the bluff around W 5th-9th St (unverified bounds).
- Further west (West End): 1900-1940 working-class frame houses: 1.5-storey gable-front cottages, foursquares, bungalows. Clapboard or asbestos/vinyl siding in white, grey, pastel (unverified).

**Hilltop / College Square (around Brady/Harrison & Locust; Palmer College, St. Ambrose nearby)**
- Hilltop Campus Village has a walkable business corridor along Harrison/Brady (unverified).
- Houses: 1880-1920 Queen Anne, foursquare, Craftsman. Many split into student flats (unverified).

**Vander Veer Park (Central Park Ave / Brady / Lombard / Harrison)**
- Park: 33-acre trapezoid with a glasshouse conservatory, rose garden, stone/concrete fountain, pond, picnic pavilion and bridge.
- District houses mostly 1895-1915: Queen Anne and Tudor Revival, also Neoclassical, Craftsman, Georgian Revival, foursquares. Large lots; the big houses line the east side of Brady.
- Harrison was formerly US 61. A Brady St streetcar once ran to Central Park.

**McClellan Heights (E River Dr to Middle Rd, Jersey Ridge Rd to Bettendorf line)**
- About 188 acres, NRHP 1984, 354 contributing buildings, built 1894-1940 on the old Camp McClellan site.
- Curving streets on hilly, ravine-cut wooded ground; odd-shaped lots. Houses were required to cost at least twice the lot price and not block neighbours' views.
- Styles: eclectic Tudor Revival, Mediterranean, Colonial/Dutch Colonial/Georgian Revival, Prairie, Craftsman, foursquare. Brick, stucco, half-timbering. Big setbacks.
- Houses on River St overlook Lindsay Park. Diedrich Busch House.

**Prospect Park area (east side, near Prospect Terrace / Mississippi Ave)** (unverified)
- Small hilltop park with river views. Surrounded by 1900-1930 foursquares and bungalows.

**Post-war rings (north of Locust to I-80)** (unverified)
- 1950s-60s ranches and split-levels. Brick or siding, attached 1-2 car garages. Curved streets, often no alleys. Lots 60-80 ft (18-24 m).

Sources: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McClellan_Heights_Historic_District; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vander_Veer_Park_Historic_District; NRHP nominations 84000328 and 85000784 (npgallery.nps.gov); hilltopcampusvillage.org; thegazette.com (Davenport brick streets mention); homes.com local guide (Near North Side alleys).

## 5. Downtown Rock Island ("The District")

- Grid of numbered avenues running east-west (1st Ave along the river, 2nd, 3rd, 4th...) and numbered streets running north-south (17th-21st St in the core). The opposite of Davenport's pattern.
- **2nd Ave, 1800 block**: a former pedestrian mall rebuilt (~$8.7M) as a **curbless "festival street"**: two travel lanes, wide (up to ~30 ft / 9 m) flush sidewalks, bollards instead of kerbs. Closed for the weekly QC Farmers' Market (approved 2025).
- 1700 block: "Arts Alley" (opened Nov 2024), a painted mural alley.
- 1st Ave = IL 92 along the river, 4-lane arterial. Centennial Bridge lands at 15th St/1st Ave (unverified).
- Buildings: 1880-1930 brick commercial blocks, 2-4 storeys, a few 6-8 storey towers, red/brown brick with limestone trim. Setback 0 m. Lots 20-40 ft.
- Brick streets: only about 8 miles left of the original network. Most bricks came from the Purington Brick Co. (Galesburg, IL). Brick Streets Plan (1988, rev. 2012): Category 1 = Highland Park HD (restore); 17 streets in Category 2 (preserve).
- Measured brick street widths: 6th Ave (17th-19th St) 31.5 ft; 8th Ave 25.3 ft; 12th St (18th-46th Ave) 31.3 ft; 21st St (3rd-6th Ave) 31.4 ft; 23rd St (2nd-3rd Ave) 37.1 ft; Highland Park 25.3-25.6 ft.
- In Highland Park most sewers follow the alleys, so rear alleys are confirmed there.
- Highland Park HD (20th-24th St x 16th-18th Ave): 1895-1928 houses on original brick streets.
- Broadway HD (17th-23rd St x 5th-7th Ave, NRHP 1998): densest set of Rock Island landmark Victorian houses. Queen Anne, Italianate, Stick (unverified styles).
- Local spots:
  - Rock Island Brewing Co. ("RIBCO", music bar): 1815 2nd Ave.
  - The Daiquiri Factory: 1809 2nd Ave.
  - The Arena (bar): 1811 2nd Ave.
  - MD Greens: 1806 2nd Ave.
  - 2nd Avenue Dance Club: 1819 2nd Ave.
  - Circa '21 Dinner Playhouse (old Fort Theatre, 1921) + Speakeasy: 1828 3rd Ave (unverified address).
  - Huckleberry's Great Pizza & Calzones: 223 18th St (unverified address).
  - Schwiebert Riverfront Park: 1st Ave at 17th St (unverified address).
  - Quad City Botanical Center: 2525 4th Ave, glass sun-garden pyramid (unverified).
  - Rock Island Arsenal + Clock Tower Building: on Arsenal Island.
  - Bally's Quad Cities Casino (old Jumer's): off I-280/IL 92 (unverified).
  - Augustana College (Old Main dome): 7th Ave/38th St (unverified).
  - Black Hawk State Historic Site + Watch Tower Lodge: south Rock Island, Blackhawk Rd (unverified).

## 7. Bettendorf

**Downtown**
- **State St and Grant St = US 67 one-way couplet**: State runs westbound along the river side, Grant eastbound (unverified directions). The core runs roughly 15th-26th St.
- A 2017 study proposed making State St a slow two-way street with parking and making Grant the two-way US 67. The city took over State St after the I-74 rebuild.
- State St streetscape and sidewalk work (17th-23rd St) started May 2026. Two-way status not confirmed.
- Buildings: small 1-2 storey 1920s-60s brick and frame commercial buildings, plus new 3-5 storey mixed-use, the Isle Casino tower and the Waterfront Convention Center. Downtown is thin and car-oriented.
- Local spots:
  - Isle Casino Hotel Bettendorf + Waterfront Convention Center: Isle Pkwy, riverfront by the I-74 bridge.
  - Ross' Restaurant (24-hour diner, "Magic Mountain"): 14th St/State St area (unverified address).
  - Family Museum + Bettendorf Library: Learning Campus Dr (unverified).
  - Lodge Hotel site (being redeveloped): Spruce Hills Dr (unverified).
  - Ascentra Credit Union HQ: between Grant and State.
  - Duck Creek Park / Duck Creek Recreational Trail: runs through Davenport and Bettendorf.

**New subdivisions (Pleasant Valley schools, Forest Grove Dr / Middle Rd / Devils Glen Rd / I-80)**
- TBK Bank Sports Complex (opened 2018) at Forest Grove Dr & Middle Rd by I-80. Indoor fieldhouse, turf fields, tall field lights; it set off retail and apartment growth (288-unit Authentix complex on Forest Grove).
- Forest Grove Dr: rebuilt and widened in phases, with roundabouts or turn lanes at Devils Glen (unverified roundabout).
- Subdivisions include Highland Ridge (SW of Forest Grove & Devils Glen), Spencer Hollow, Beaver Crossing, Devils Bluff.
- 1990s+ subdivision archetype (unverified, typical of Iowa growth suburbs):
  - Curving streets and cul-de-sacs (bulb radius ~15-18 m). Streets 26-31 ft (8-9.5 m), poured concrete with integral curb and gutter.
  - Utilities buried, with green pedestal boxes and transformers on front or rear lot lines. No poles.
  - Lots 70-100 ft (21-30 m) wide, front setback 25-30 ft (7.6-9 m).
  - Houses: 1-1.5 storey ranches with walkout basements on slopes; 2-storey "Midwest traditional" with garage-forward facades and 2-3 car attached garages (3-car common).
  - Exterior: vinyl or LP SmartSide lap siding (greige, tan, grey, white, now dark blue/charcoal), stone or brick veneer wainscot on the front, white trim, asphalt architectural shingles (weathered wood/charcoal), 6/12-8/12 roofs with several gables.
  - Concrete driveways 6-9 m wide. Small young trees, sod, lots of backyard decks. Sidewalks 1.5 m (5 ft) on both sides.
  - Mailboxes in cluster units on newer plats (unverified).

## 9. Geneseo and Le Claire

**Geneseo, IL (Henry Co., "Maple City")**
- Downtown around a central green, **City Park**, on the site of the old Geneseo Seminary / Township High School. It is not a courthouse square: the county seat is Cambridge (unverified seat).
- State St = main north-south commercial street. Exchange St = east-west street at the square (unverified, found no source).
- 19th-c. 2-3 storey brick Italianate commercial blocks around the park, plus large Victorian and Queen Anne houses on tree-lined maple streets. Bandstand or gazebo in the park (unverified).
- Spots: Geneseo Historical Museum, 205 S State St; Geneseo Brewing Co., downtown (unverified address); Hennepin Canal Parkway just south (unverified).

**Le Claire, IA**
- Main street = **Cody Rd (US 67 / Great River Road)**, parallel to the river. Cody Road Historic District (NRHP 1979): ~60 buildings over 9 blocks / 20 acres. Commercial at the south end, residential at the north.
- Land drops steeply east to the river, so river-side buildings show exposed basements. Wooded limestone bluffs rise to the west.
- Eras 1850-1900. Commercial = narrow 2-storey brick blocks, 2-3 bays wide, flat roofs with parapets and metal cornices, built of soft local brick on local limestone foundations.
- Houses: Greek Revival, Italianate, Queen Anne, Eastlake, Shingle; wood or brick.
- Tallest building: Bard Hotel, 3 storeys, 207 N Cody Rd. Old Le Claire City Hall: 201-203 N Cody Rd.
- Spots:
  - Mississippi River Distilling Co.: 303 N Cody Rd.
  - Green Tree Brewery: 309 N Cody Rd.
  - Antique Archaeology (Mike Wolfe's shop from American Pickers): side street off Cody Rd, 115 Davenport St (unverified address).
  - Buffalo Bill Museum: N Front St on the levee, with the stern-wheeler *Lone Star* (unverified address).
  - Riverboat Twilight cruises; Le Claire Park/levee; Steamboat Days (unverified).

## 10. Farmland and farmsteads (Scott Co. IA / Rock Island & Henry Co. IL)

- Land grid: US Public Land Survey. Section-line roads every 1 mile (1.609 km) north-south and east-west. Fields are mostly whole or part 40/80/160-acre quarter sections. Rolling ground near rivers; flat to gently rolling upland.
- Crops: corn/soybean rotation, about 50/50 across years. 30-inch rows (0.76 m).
  - Corn: 2.4-3 m tall July-Sept, tan when dry Sept-Oct, stubble Nov-Apr.
  - Soybeans: 0.6-1 m, bright green, turning yellow-gold in late Sept.
  - Some hay/pasture and CRP grass strips along creeks (unverified).
- Roads:
  - Scott County maintains 562 mi: 324 mi gravel, 225 mi paved (some "county dirt"). So about 58% of county roads are gravel.
  - Gravel roads are crushed limestone, light grey-white, 6-7 m wide, crowned, with dust plumes (unverified width).
  - Paved county roads are asphalt or concrete, 2 lanes, 6.7-7.3 m (22-24 ft), yellow centre line, white edge lines, narrow gravel shoulders (unverified).
- Ditches: grassy V or trapezoid ditches each side, 1-2 m deep, 3-6 m wide. Field entrances cross over corrugated metal culverts (unverified dims).
- Power lines: single-phase wood poles on one side of most county roads, 60-90 m apart, set in the right-of-way just inside the field fence line. A drop to each farmstead (unverified).
- Mailbox: a single US-style box on a post at the end of the driveway, on the right side of the carrier's route. Name or number painted on it. Blue/white or green rural address (fire number) sign on a post (unverified Scott Co. sign colour).
- Farmstead layout: a 1-4 ha cluster set back 50-200 m from the road down a gravel lane, with a **windbreak** of conifers (red cedar, spruce, pine) on the north and west sides, plus a few big deciduous shade trees around the house (unverified species).
- Buildings:
  - House: white (or pale) 2-storey frame farmhouse (I-house, foursquare, or gabled-ell with porch); newer farms have 1960s-2000s ranch houses.
  - Barn: red gable or gambrel-roof barn, often with white trim and a white X on the doors. Only about 60,000 of Iowa's 200,000 barns are left, many in poor shape. Some white barns.
  - Machine sheds: steel pole buildings, 12-24 m wide x 24-60 m long, 5-6 m eaves, grey/white/tan corrugated walls with coloured trim and big sliding or overhead doors (unverified dims).
  - Grain bins: corrugated galvanized steel cylinders, 2-6 grouped. 6-14 m diameter, 8-20 m tall, cone roofs. A bucket-elevator leg and dryer tower beside them (unverified dims).
  - Silos: concrete-stave (grey) or clay-block "Iowa silo". **Blue Harvestore silos** (glass-lined steel, cobalt blue, from the 1970s) on dairy and beef farms, about 6-9 m diameter x 18-25 m tall (unverified dims).
  - Corncribs and hog and poultry houses are mostly gone. Modern hog confinements are long low white or tan steel barns with curtain sides (unverified).
- Small towns: grain elevator (tall concrete silos plus metal bins) by the railroad (unverified).

Sources: scottcountyiowa.gov/roads/roads-bridges; preservationiowa.org (farmsteads and barns); timesrepublican.com 2019 (why barns are red); iowabarnfoundation.org Fall 2022 silo history (Harvestore blue); iowahistoryjournal.com (barns fading).

## 11. General Iowa/Illinois street practice (renderer rules)

- MUTCD applies in both states.
  - Yellow line = separates opposing traffic (double solid yellow on arterials, dashed yellow where passing is allowed on rural roads).
  - White = lane lines (dashed 10 ft line / 30 ft gap = 3 m / 9 m), edge lines, stop bars, crosswalks.
- Residential local streets: no centre line, no edge lines. Stop bars usually absent at residential stop signs. Stop bars and crosswalks are standard at signals (unverified "usually").
- Crosswalks: newer and school crossings use continental/ladder bars (white, ~0.6 m bars with 0.6-1.5 m gaps). Older downtown crossings use two parallel lines. Davenport's downtown standard is painted, 10 ft (3.05 m) wide, with no special paving (bar style not stated).
- Stop signs: octagon, 30 in (0.76 m) on local streets and 36 in on arterials, on the right corner. Bottom about 7 ft (2.1 m) above the sidewalk in town, 5 ft rural. Iowa and Illinois towns often add a red "4-WAY" plaque. Street-name blades are usually mounted on the same post above the stop sign (unverified local practice).
- Street-name signs: MUTCD green with white letters is the norm in Davenport, Bettendorf, Moline and Rock Island (unverified, found no source). Brown or other custom signs may appear in historic districts (unverified). Overhead mast-arm name signs at signals, sometimes with block numbers (unverified).
- Signals: yellow or black heads on galvanized or black mast arms; downtown Davenport black (unverified).
- Lighting: cobra-head LED on galvanized/wood poles on arterials. Black decorative poles downtown (Davenport replacing cobra heads). Residential lights are often on the wood utility poles (unverified).
- Power poles: wood, 10-14 m tall. Urban spans 35-45 m; rural 60-90 m (unverified).
- Fire hydrants: no Davenport or Moline source found. Common Iowa practice is a red or yellow body with caps colour-coded by flow under NFPA 291 (red <500 gpm, orange 500-999, green 1000-1499, light blue 1500+). Davenport (Iowa American Water) is red/silver-capped (unverified); Moline is yellow (unverified).
- US residential street widths: local streets 26-36 ft (7.9-11 m) curb to curb, ROW 50-60 ft (15-18 m), sidewalks 4-5 ft (1.2-1.5 m), parkway 4-8 ft (1.2-2.4 m). Rock Island code: 25 ft paving with parking on one side, 27 ft with parking on both sides (minimums, from the brick plan).
- Kerb: 6 in (15 cm) concrete curb and gutter. Downtown Davenport has a maximum 6 in curb height.
- Arterial sections:
  - 2-lane: 2 x 3.35-3.6 m (11-12 ft) lanes.
  - 3-lane: 1+1 + 3.6-4.3 m centre turn lane (Davenport 3rd St).
  - 5-lane: 2+2 + centre turn lane, 18-20 m curb to curb (E 53rd St).
  - Divided 4-6 lane: raised median 3-6 m (Kimberly, parts of River Dr) (unverified widths).
- Snow country details: wide concrete roads (Iowa uses lots of PCC concrete), salt-stained kerbs, snow-route signs (red/white "Snow Emergency Route") on Davenport arterials (unverified).

Sources: MUTCD 2009/2023 (mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov) (general standards); Davenport Streetscape Plan 2018; Rock Island Brick Streets Plan rev 2012; blog.qrfs.com and wwdmag.com (NFPA 291 hydrant colours); cityofhastings.org (hydrant colour variation).

## How the game uses these notes (src/city/areas.js, tile.js, detail.js)

- **Area of every building / street**, from data: town core (downtown Davenport's bounds, plus a core circle at each town's real main street: East Davenport, Rock Island, Moline, Bettendorf, East Moline, Silvis, Milan, Le Claire, Port Byron, Geneseo, Colona, Coal Valley...), industrial (building type, kind or `landuse=industrial` ground), farmland (OSM fields / farmyards, or near-empty land well outside a town), strip (retail/commercial type or a big flat box off the core), else houses.
- **House era**: OSM `start_date` when tagged, else the historic districts above (Gold Coast/Hamburg 1870s, Hilltop 1890s, Vander Veer 1905, McClellan Heights and Prospect Park 1915, West End 1910, Highland Park RI 1910, Broadway RI 1885, the Village 1870), else distance from the nearest core (founding years at the core, ~1935 at the edge of the pre-war grid, then ~100 m per year outward), at least 1968 on curving streets / cul-de-sacs.
- **House style** from era + footprint: pre-1900 big or complex footprint = Victorian (steep roof, wrap porch, turret on complex footprints), small = gable-front cottage; 1900-30 square = foursquare (hip roof, dormer, full porch), else bungalow (low roof, dormer, tapered posts); 1946-77 = ranch (attached garage door, hip roof on bigger ones); 1978+ big = two-storey with 2-car garage, stone wainscot, no front fence. OSM `building:levels`, `roof:shape`, `building:material`, `building:colour` override.
- **Utilities**: OSM power lines/poles where mapped; else down the alleys in the pre-war grid, one side of the street elsewhere, none in post-1975 subdivisions and downtowns (buried), every ~68 m on county roads.
- **Roads**: OSM surface (brick, gravel), sidewalk and lit tags; untagged local roads out in the country are gravel at the Scott County rate (~58%). Grass parkway between kerb and sidewalk in neighbourhoods, a brick kerb strip on downtown's bigger streets, painted crosswalks on every leg in a core.
